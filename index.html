QLD SEQ Public Transport Interactive Map

Unofficial product. This map is not produced, endorsed or maintained by Translink or the Queensland Government. It is an independent visualisation of Translink open data, provided for information and consultation only. Routes, stops and services change — always check translink.com.au or the MyTranslink app before travelling. No warranty is given as to accuracy or completeness.

Bus route numbering zones show where each three-digit route series predominantly stops (a rule of thumb, not exact boundaries).

Striped areas and outliers: each zone is drawn where its route series makes up most of the stops. A striped area covers the 500 m around stops that are either shared by more than one series (stripes in each series' colour) or are outliers: stops served by a series but sitting more than about 400 m outside that series' zone, such as a route running out to a small town. Each outlier patch has a dotted line back to its zone along the road the bus actually takes, including routes where only some trips go that far. Tap a dotted line to see which route it is and when it runs.

How to use: search a route to see its path and stops in order; search a station, stop, street or suburb to jump there. Tap any station or stop (bus stops appear when zoomed in) to see every route serving it with its next departure and the rest of today's scheduled times (scroll sideways); tap a route badge to open that route. Show all routes draws every route from that stop, with white arrows showing the direction of travel away from it; tap any line or route to open it, and use ‹ to return to the stop. Each button under the search bars cycles through on, off and only this layer (a third click brings the other layers back as they were); the half-circle button switches between light and dark maps. Tap ☆ on a stop or route to save it; ★ Stops and ★ Routes show your saved ones (saved on this device only). Saved routes open a full timetable with every stop, Earlier/Later and a day picker, plus live vehicles and updates. Under each destination is the platform or stop it leaves from; amber "Now platform …" means the live feed shows a platform change. In the Next column, green times are live predictions, amber means running late, and grey is the scheduled time.

What the colours mean: busways are purple (Northern), teal (South East) and orange (Eastern); thinner sections run on ordinary roads. Striped zone areas are where stops from more than one route series overlap. The ⚠ Alerts button turns orange when Translink has posted alerts, tagged Disruption (unplanned, including cancelled trips) or Planned works. Your saved stops and routes, and the ★ buttons, turn orange when an alert affects them, red when a trip has been cancelled and hasn't started yet (red clears once its start time passes), and half red, half orange for both. Fleet numbers also show on saved-route timetables and under live times on stop cards. Journey Search (top bar, or Connections on a station card) finds journeys between stations, stops, places and street addresses (official G-NAF addresses; businesses aren't listed, and Search online appears only when nothing matches). Walks follow real streets and paths, with an adjustable walking distance (up to 2 km), an Avoid steps switch, and a walk-all-the-way option when walking is close or quickest. Walking › on a journey opens each walk's distance, climb, elevation profile and flags; an orange ! means a steep stretch or steps. Flags follow AS 1428.1 and the Disability Standards for Accessible Public Transport: steeper than an accessible ramp is over 1 in 14 (7.1%, 4.1°), steeper than a kerb ramp is over 1 in 8 (12.5%, 7.1°), and over 1 in 4 (25%, 14°) is beyond assisted access. "1 in 14" means rising 1 m for every 14 m along. Only stretches of about 30 m or longer are flagged. Heights are the Queensland Government's LiDAR of the bare ground: the map keeps its own copy for everywhere within 2 km of a stop (updated when Queensland republishes it) and asks Queensland's service for anywhere further out. Satellite heights: only if neither is available does a walk use satellite heights (Amazon's open terrain tiles, 30 m data). These include buildings and trees, so they can show a city block as a steep hill; the Walking card then says so and marks only steps, not the climb or steep stretches. Paths under cover or through buildings aren't measured either, as bare-ground heights aren't known there. It's a guide, not official accessibility advice. Brisbane bus stop cards also have ♿ Accessibility: Brisbane City Council's ratings (easy, medium or hard) for the boarding point, the slope along the road and the sideways slope, plus tactile indicators and when the stop was last audited; Journey Search tags stops whose boarding point is rated hard. ♿ Accessibility on train station cards shows Queensland Rail's access rating (independent, assisted or limited access), its lifts, ramps, stairs and boarding details, and links to the station's access map, printable map and audio description (checked weekly), plus any lift outage in today's Translink alerts. Journey Search tags stations on a journey that are rated assisted or limited access or have a lift out of service, and walks can use lifts mapped in OpenStreetMap to get around steps (the Walking card says so). Locality map ↗ on a station card opens Translink's locality map for that station, when there is one. Cross River Rail and The Wave (More ▾) clear the map like On demand and draw each project's sites: Cross River Rail's tunnel route, stations and work sites, and The Wave's planned rail corridor (Beerwah to Birtinya), metro route (Birtinya to Sunshine Coast Airport) and related road works. Tap a site for what the government, TMR and the delivery authority have published about it, including Cross River Rail's latest progress and works notices (updated weekly), then ‹ to go back. The Wave's corridor is traced from TMR's published maps, so it's approximate; dashed lines are stretches that couldn't be placed exactly, or that TMR is still investigating. Each card ends with links to the official sites, and any of today's service alerts caused by the project (naming it, or works at its stations) show at the top of its cards. Route and train line cards show the platform or stop letter used at each stop. Saved stops open collapsed; tap + to show a stop's departures. Bus zones start off on your first visit; your layer choices are remembered after that. Commuter Catch-Ups (More ▾) lists Queensland Rail's upcoming drop-in sessions at stations, with contact details and links. Measured on route and train line cards is how long services actually took from the first stop, recorded from Translink's live data every day in the AM (6:30–9:30 am) and PM (3:30–6:30 pm) windows; the cards use weekdays (school term and school holidays) over the last four weeks, as a median. It shows the AM figure before noon and the PM figure after; the small AM/PM switch above the list changes it. Orange means 2 min or more slower than timetabled, and ≈ marks stops after a stretch with no recordings yet. Real time average (More ▾) lists every route by AM or PM and direction, with an overview and stop-by-stop times, and Delay heat map clears the map like On demand and colours each stretch between stops from clear (keeps to time) to dark red (furthest behind timetable), with All, AM and PM filters. Both have a period menu (Live = past 7 days, Last 4 weeks, All time, a month, a range of months, or specific days up to 92) and a days menu (Every day, or any mix of school-term weekdays, school-holiday weekdays, weekends and public holidays; weekdays by default). Months are kept for 12 months; all time keeps everything since recording began. Single days, months, live and the last 4 weeks are medians; all time, ranges and mixes of days combine them, weighted by trips. On phones the top bar is one row you can swipe sideways (Alerts, Layers, Saved, Search, BAU, More); Layers, Saved and Search open as drop-downs, and Journey Search is under Search. Cards open over about three-quarters of the screen (the left half when the phone is sideways); drag the handle or title to resize, – shrinks a card to its title bar and ✕ closes it. Notes (More ▾) keeps your own notes in this browser on this device only (nothing is sent anywhere, and they don't sync): each is titled by its first 25 characters (then …) unless you type a title of up to 25 characters in the box at the top; Export ▾ saves every note as a .json backup or a .csv spreadsheet, Copy all copies them as text, Import brings back a .json backup or a .txt note, and the bin on a note or Delete all removes notes after a check. Each note has its own Export ▾ (.json or .csv), Copy and Delete, and starts with a counter: name it for anything you want to count, then tap − or + or type a number in (+ Add counter adds more; Reset and the bin ask first). Counters are included in Copy and exports. Clearing the browser's website data deletes them, so export now and then. Rail replacement buses (More ▾) lists planned train closures from Translink's published timetable, about four weeks ahead and checked every morning; the menu item turns orange while buses are replacing trains and light blue when a closure is coming up. It clears the map like On demand and draws the closed stretch of track as a grey dashed line with its stations named, and the replacement buses in orange with their stops (temporary stops have a thicker ring); tap a closure for its dates and hours, buses and stations, and a route for its timetable. Station cards say when buses replace trains there in the next week. The closed stretch is worked out from where the buses run, so check Translink's service updates before travelling. More ▾ holds extras: School routes lists school-only buses, which can share numbers with everyday routes. Translink journey planner ↗ (More ▾) opens Translink's official journey planner in a new tab. On demand shades Translink's on-demand bus areas in navy, with yellow dots for their destinations, and hides the other layers while it's on; tap an area for details. Ipswich, Bribie Island and Yarrabilba areas are traced from Translink's map pictures, so their edges are approximate. go card vendors puts Translink's retailers and station ticket offices (orange) and fare machines (grey) on the map, also hiding the other layers; filter by what you want to do, by card type or to fare machines only, and tap a dot to see what that location offers. The – button shrinks the vendor card instead of closing it; tap its title to bring it back. These areas update themselves when Translink publishes a new map. If a new map can't be traced confidently, the previous area stays on the map and an amber note on the On demand card says so. The – button shrinks On demand cards instead of closing them, and ‹ takes you from an area back to the full list.

Dotted lines and variants: dotted lines link outlying stops back to their zone along the real bus path; tap one to see the route, whether only some trips run that way, and today's times. Route cards list a route's variants under Some trips also run via and draw them dashed; the full timetable shows every trip, with a dash where a trip doesn't stop.

Following a live service: tap a moving bus, train, ferry or tram on the map, or tap a route on a stop or station card (it follows the next live service on that row). The route card shows its fleet number (trains show a service number, ferries the vessel name) and, beside each stop, that service's scheduled time, minutes from its first stop, and a status updated every 20 seconds:

DepartedThe service has already been through this stop; the stop is greyed out. ArrivingThe service is within 500 m of this stop. Stations close together can both show Arriving at once. Not arrivedThe stop is still ahead and the service is more than 500 m away.

Statuses come from the vehicle's own GPS position and next stop in Translink's live feed. If a service isn't running yet, the route opens without them.

Updates: routes, timetables, bus zones and busways refresh automatically every week from Translink's data, once changes are actually running. How it works and how to update it by hand (for example adding a new busway station) is explained on the project page.

Timetable data (scheduled times, Brisbane time): Translink GTFS feed of (route paths simplified). Live vehicle positions, arrival predictions, cancellations and service alerts: Translink GTFS-Realtime, refreshed every 15–30 seconds.
